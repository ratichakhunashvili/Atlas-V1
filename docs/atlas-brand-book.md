## Brand at a glance

ATLAS helps travelers move beyond a checklist of attractions into a personal journey through local culture, people, places, and experiences. Hotels introduce guests to ATLAS; the platform recommends relevant experiences; local partners provide bookable activities; curated quests and hotel rewards encourage discovery.  
The visual system must make this experience feel trustworthy, intelligent, calm, human, and internationally ready. It is premium without being distant and technological without feeling cold.  

## Brand promise

Make every stay a starting point for a story.  

## Brand essence

A personal gateway to local stories.  

## Official slogan

All trips leave a story.  

## Product logic

- Explore — discover experiences shaped by interests, budget, time, and location.
- Experience — visit a place, meet a host, take part in an activity, or complete a curated quest.
- Unlock — collect a memory, achievement, or participating hotel reward.
The product should make discovery feel simple. The brand should make it feel meaningful.  


## 01. Brand foundation


### Purpose

Help people find meaningful local experiences and help local hospitality and tourism businesses connect with the travelers who will value them.  

### Mission

Connect hotels, travelers, and local experience providers through personalized discovery, curated activities, QR-based access, and rewarding journeys.  

### Vision

A world where a hotel stay opens a door to authentic local stories — and where travelers leave with more than a list of places visited.  

### Values

- Curiosity: encourage exploration beyond the obvious.
- Trust: make recommendations, pricing, partner details, and next steps clear.
- Hospitality: treat every traveler as a guest, not a conversion metric.
- Local connection: represent places and people with respect.
- Intelligence: personalize help without overwhelming or intruding.
- Meaningful progress: make quests and rewards useful, not childish.


## 02. Positioning

ATLAS sits at the intersection of premium hospitality, travel technology, personalized discovery, and local cultural experiences.  
**Positioning statement:** For modern travelers who want to understand a destination rather than merely pass through it, ATLAS is a hotel-connected local experience platform that turns a guest's available time, interests, and budget into a relevant journey. Unlike generic lists of attractions, ATLAS connects recommendations to local providers, experiences, quests, and participating hotel rewards.  

### Primary audiences

- International travelers exploring Georgia and other destinations.
- Travelers seeking authentic culture, food, nature, heritage, wellness, and local encounters.
- Guests who value convenient, tailored recommendations.

### Business audiences

- Boutique hotels and hotel groups.
- Local tour operators, attractions, activity providers, restaurants, guides, and cultural businesses.
- Tourism organizations and strategic partners.
### What ATLAS is

- A personal gateway to local experiences
- A helpful layer within a hotel stay
- A bridge between visitors and local providers
- A clear, modern digital guide
- A way to discover and unlock experiences

### What ATLAS is not

- A generic booking directory
- A replacement for a hotel's hospitality
- A platform that treats local culture as decoration
- A childish game or a loud AI gadget
- A promise that every trip will be life-changing


## 03. Creative direction — Intelligent Elegance

**The design language is Intelligent Elegance:** controlled geometry, confident typography, generous space, and restrained cartographic movement.  

### Visual principles

- Precise: alignment, proportions, and spacing should feel intentional.
- Open: use negative space to make important content easy to understand.
- Human: balance interface clarity with real people, places, materials, and light.
- Distinctive: the final ATLAS mark and Path System should be recognizable without relying on stock travel clichés.
- Restrained: use bronze as a signal, not as a dominant decoration.
- Adaptable: the identity must work across mobile screens, hotel collateral, social channels, print, and investor material.
- Avoid airplane symbols, generic map pins, stock compass roses, childish illustrations, heavy gradients, neon glow, excessive gold effects, crowded layouts, and ornamental graphics with no role.


## 04. Final logo — approved master artwork

The uploaded ATLAS symbol is the final master mark for this edition. Preserve its geometry: a strong geometric “A”, a deliberately open orbit-like arc, and a small bronze discovery star.  

### Meaning of the mark

**The A:** a clear, confident anchor for the ATLAS name.  
**The open orbit:** orientation, movement, and an ongoing journey; the path is intentionally not closed.  
**The star:** a point of discovery — the moment a destination becomes a story.  
**Negative space:** keeps the mark elegant and legible at a range of sizes.  
This explanation is a design rationale for the supplied symbol, not a claim that it depicts a literal compass or exact geographic route.  

### Artwork source and color discipline

The master file is the provided logo artwork. The supplied raster source samples to a navy-teal mark of #083A4F and a star of #C17D57, on a warm background of #EADFD4. Keep the master artwork intact. The established ATLAS interface and brand palette remains #0a4a4f, #d7c4b1, and #a58d66. Use the approved transparent master for primary placement; use the supplied reverse or monochrome variants only for situations where contrast requires it.  
Do not redraw the orbit, change the star shape, round the letterform, close the orbit, or move the star independently. Do not apply 3D, bevels, drop shadows, outlines, glow, or arbitrary gradients to the logo.  

### Logo configurations

- Primary symbol: final uploaded mark with transparent background.
- Reverse symbol: light mark for approved dark backgrounds; retain the star accent where contrast allows.
- Monochrome dark: single-color mark for one-ink or reduced-color production.
- Monochrome light: single-color mark for dark surfaces.
- App icon: the symbol centered inside the ATLAS Deep Teal rounded-square field.
- Wordmark lockup: pair the symbol with the uppercase ATLAS wordmark and the official slogan where a complete brand signature is needed.
- The icon is the fixed artwork. The wordmark and slogan are typeset lockup elements unless supplied as a final outlined/vector master.

### Clear space

Use the star's diameter as a practical X unit. Keep at least 1X clear around the outermost edge of the full symbol, including the orbit and star. For a wordmark lockup, apply at least 1X around the complete combined unit.  

### Minimum size guidance

- App / interface icon: design for 24 px and test at 16 px before release.
- Print symbol: 8 mm minimum height for standard print; enlarge for textured or low-quality stock.
- Full logo lockup: aim for at least 32 mm wide in print where the slogan is included.
- These are production starting points; always test actual small-size rendering because the orbit and star are important recognition details.

### Backgrounds

- Use the master logo on light, quiet backgrounds.
- Use the reverse logo on Deep Teal or approved charcoal surfaces.
- Place the logo on photography only when a quiet, high-contrast area exists.
- Never put the logo on a high-detail part of an image.

### Incorrect usage

Never stretch, skew, rotate, crop away part of the orbit, delete or relocate the star, alter the symbol's internal proportions, substitute an unrelated icon, add effects, or put it on a low-contrast background. Do not type a substitute “A” and present it as the approved symbol.  


## 05. Color system

The three established colors remain fixed. Supporting neutrals are functional, not additional dominant brand colors.  
| Color | HEX | Role | Recommended use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deep Teal | #0a4a4f | Primary | Main brand surfaces, primary UI, headlines, primary buttons |
| Warm Beige | #d7c4b1 | Secondary | Editorial backgrounds, cards, print, hospitality materials |
| Muted Bronze | #a58d66 | Accent | Thin rules, key markers, selected highlights, premium details |
| Charcoal | #1d2929 | Supporting neutral | Long-form text on light surfaces, limited dark UI surfaces |
| Warm White | #f6f3ed | Supporting neutral | Clean page background, light cards, clear breathing space |


### Color proportions

- 60–70% Warm White / Warm Beige across light editorial and content-heavy layouts.
- 20–35% Deep Teal for structure, strong brand moments, and primary actions.
- 0–8% Muted Bronze for accents only.
- On dark, immersive applications, Deep Teal can become the dominant surface while Warm Beige carries the text and panels.

### Preferred pairings

- Deep Teal text on Warm Beige or Warm White.
- Warm Beige text on Deep Teal.
- Charcoal text on Warm White.
- Bronze for small accents beside high-contrast text, not as the sole color of small body copy.

### Accessibility

Use Deep Teal for primary text on light surfaces and Warm Beige or Warm White for text on Deep Teal. Do not use bronze alone for small text or essential control states. Test text, icons, buttons, and interactive states against WCAG contrast targets during product implementation; color values alone do not guarantee accessibility at every size and weight.  

### Print

Use the HEX values as digital reference. For print, create and proof CMYK or spot-color equivalents with the chosen printer and paper stock. Warm Beige and Muted Bronze can shift noticeably across uncoated paper, coated paper, and screen.  


## 06. Typography system

Use open-licensed type families and test Georgian and Latin strings in the actual products, documents, and exports.  

### Primary Latin family — Space Grotesk

Use Space Grotesk for the uppercase wordmark setting, selected headlines, navigation, category labels, and concise interface text. It combines geometric precision with a distinctive, contemporary voice. The family is published under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Official source: Space Grotesk project.  

### Secondary Latin family — Manrope

Use Manrope for body text and longer interface copy when a softer, calm reading texture is useful. Keep its role consistent; do not randomly alternate Manrope and Space Grotesk inside the same level of hierarchy. The family is published under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Official source: Manrope on Google Fonts.  

### Georgian family — Noto Georgian

**Noto Sans Georgian:** interface, labels, body copy, data, and functional text.  
**Noto Serif Georgian:** selected editorial headlines or emotional statements where a serif voice suits the content.  
The Noto Georgian project publishes both families under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 and includes current family files and specimens: Noto Georgian project and Noto Georgian specimens.  
Space Grotesk and Manrope do not provide the required Georgian glyph coverage in the relevant Latin font releases; use the Noto Georgian families for Georgian script instead of relying on a system fallback. Validate every chosen file and version in the final deployment environment.  

### Recommended type scale

| Token | Desktop starting point | Mobile starting point | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | 56–72 px | 36–44 px | Opening statements, hero headline |
| H1 | 40–48 px | 30–36 px | Page title |
| H2 | 28–32 px | 24–28 px | Major section heading |
| H3 | 20–24 px | 18–22 px | Card and subsection heading |
| Body large | 18–20 px | 17–18 px | Introductory paragraph |
| Body | 16–18 px | 16 px | Main readable copy |
| Label / caption | 12–14 px | 12–14 px | UI label, metadata, caption |

These are starting tokens; follow platform conventions and accessibility testing.  

### Typography rules

- Use regular or medium weights for most body text; reserve bold for hierarchy, not emphasis on every line.
- Use uppercase with restrained tracking for short labels, not for paragraphs.
- Use sentence case for UI actions and explanatory copy.
- Keep long text left-aligned; use centered typography mainly for covers, short statements, or deliberate ceremonial moments.
- Use consistent line height: roughly 1.45–1.65 for body text, 1.1–1.25 for large headlines.
- Do not depend on color alone to create hierarchy.
- Never substitute a different font just for a single heading without updating the system.


## 07. The ATLAS Path System — signature graphics

The Path System extends the logo's open orbit into an ownable family of routes, intersections, and discovery points. It is a navigational graphic language, not generic topographic decoration.  

### A. Continuous Path — primary pattern

One calm continuous line travels through a composition, changes direction with a limited number of curves, and passes selected nodes. Use this pattern for cover pages, section dividers, website hero bands, and hotel welcome cards.  

### B. Discovery Grid — supporting pattern

A restrained geometric field provides intersections and selected points of emphasis. Use it for product explanation slides, process diagrams, event graphics, or layouts where a sense of system and orientation helps.  

### C. Destination Field — supporting pattern

Large arcs, cropped contours, and open spaces suggest an area still to be discovered. Use it as a quiet image overlay, card background, or social-media frame.  

### Pattern rules

- Use consistent stroke weights within each pattern family.
- Use low opacity on content-heavy backgrounds; the pattern must remain subordinate to text and imagery.
- Let lines enter and leave the frame deliberately; cropping should feel intentional.
- Keep destination nodes rare and purposeful.
- Align the pattern's open arcs and star-like accents with the character of the final logo.
- Do not pass a path through headlines, critical instructions, QR codes, or calls to action.
- Do not decorate every surface. One well-placed pattern is more distinctive than a full-page texture on every screen.

### Pattern use by surface

| Surface | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Presentation cover | Low-opacity Continuous Path, ample negative space |
| Brand book editorial page | Small cropped route detail or one quiet node |
| QR experience card | Short path leading the eye toward the QR area; keep QR clear |
| Website hero | Large cropped arc behind or beside the headline |
| Social post | A single edge line or distinctive node, not a pattern collage |
| Hotel welcome card | Minimal path detail around the perimeter |
| Digital app interface | Use only to indicate flow or progress, not as a background texture |


## 08. Photography and art direction

Photography should feel authentic, cinematic, warm, human, and culturally grounded. Show the experience of a destination, not only its famous viewpoint.  

### Prioritize

- Architectural details, material textures, and understated hotel spaces.
- Travelers genuinely discovering a place.
- Local hosts, craftspeople, guides, cooks, and experience providers represented with dignity.
- Natural light, shadows, weather, and real atmosphere.
- Human interaction and quiet moments alongside landscapes.
- Georgian places and culture with accurate, respectful context as the initial market focus.

### Color and composition

- Natural warm light; avoid orange-heavy filters.
- Deep Teal and Warm Beige should integrate naturally, not be forced over every photograph.
- Bronze can appear in graphic overlays, not as a metallic image filter.
- Leave negative space where titles or UI overlays will sit.
- Favor one strong scene over stock-photo collages.
- Use restrained crops and consistent tonal treatment across a campaign.

### Avoid

- Over-saturated colors, exaggerated luxury clichés, generic airplane-window stock photos, artificial HDR, tourist crowds used as filler, culturally inaccurate staging, and images that stereotype local communities.


## 09. Brand voice

ATLAS speaks with calm confidence and genuine curiosity. It helps people choose the next step without making every destination sound extraordinary or every interaction sound automated.  

### Voice attributes

- Inspiring, not exaggerated.
- Sophisticated, not pretentious.
- Helpful, not robotic.
- Curious, not intrusive.
- Warm, not overfamiliar.
- Culturally respectful, not exoticizing.
- Clear, not overexplained.

### Preferred vocabulary

- Discover · Explore · Experience · Local · Story · Nearby · Curated · Unlock · Meet · Taste · Learn · Wander · Your time · Your interests

### Avoid or use carefully

- Empty superlatives such as “the best ever” or “once-in-a-lifetime” unless substantiated.
- Pushy urgency such as “Book now or miss out”.
- Generic tech terms when plain language works better.
- Claims that every local interaction is authentic or guaranteed to be unforgettable.
- Copy that treats residents as props for a tourist's experience.

## 11. Digital product identity

The digital product should be clean, useful, and confidence-building. Brand graphics support orientation; they do not replace navigation or content structure.  

### Core UI principles

- One clear primary action per screen.
- Make price, duration, location, availability, and key conditions easy to find.
- Present personalized recommendations as suggestions with transparent reasons, not as mysterious AI decisions.
- Keep progress and rewards useful, calm, and adult.
- Use Journey Lines to indicate a path or sequence only when they aid understanding.
- Respect privacy and consent; private memories are private by default.
- Support readable type sizes, touch targets, keyboard navigation, and contrast.

### Application icon

Use the final symbol centered in a Deep Teal rounded-square field with appropriate breathing room. At very small sizes, test whether the open orbit and bronze star remain recognizable. If not, use a simplified production micro-variant approved for small-size use, without altering the master logo artwork.  

### Interface components

**Experience card:** image, title, distance/location, duration, price, and a single action.  
**Quest progress:** clear status, steps completed, next action, and reward conditions.  
**Reward card:** provider, value, redemption instructions, and expiry/limitations when applicable.  
**Map and route:** labels, accessible list alternatives, and the destination context; avoid route graphics that obscure the map.  
**AI assistant:** clear input, helpful suggestions, explicit confirmation for any action that changes a booking or preference.  


## 12. Hotel, QR, quest, and reward applications


### Hotel welcome card

**Front:** final symbol, ATLAS wordmark, All trips leave a story.  
**Back:** a short invitation, QR code, short URL fallback, and brief privacy note if personal data is requested.  

### QR experience card

Keep the code in a quiet, high-contrast area.  
Do not place the Path System behind the QR code.  
Include a short instruction and a human-readable URL fallback.  
Test printed codes on the actual stock and size.  

### Quest card

Use a concise title, clear steps, realistic completion conditions, and an explicit reward description. Avoid excessive badges, cartoon effects, or competitive language that makes local culture feel like a game prop.  

### Reward state

Show what is unlocked, who honors it, how to redeem it, restrictions, and expiry. Never imply a reward is valid before a partner confirms the terms.  


## 13. Social media identity

All social channels share the same logo, color palette, typography, and signature graphic family. Adjust the content layout to each platform rather than stretching one template into every format.  

### Content pillars

- Local stories: people, craft, food, traditions, and context.
- Experience discovery: specific, useful activities and places.
- Traveler moments: real stories and memories, used with permission.
- Partner stories: hotels and providers, described accurately.
- Behind ATLAS: product progress, learning, and milestones.

### Post structure

- One clear main idea.
- A strong image or deliberate graphic composition.
- Short, readable headline.
- One next step where appropriate.
- Consistent brand color and typographic hierarchy.
- No unsupported performance numbers, testimonial claims, or partnership statements.

### Example social headline

Your stay is a starting point.  
Explore nearby experiences that make a destination feel personal.  


## 14. Website and discovery experience

The website should introduce the concept quickly and direct visitors to the relevant path: traveler, hotel, or local provider.  

### Recommended information hierarchy

- Hero: Discover places. Unlock experiences. Leave with stories.
- How it works: hotel QR → personal preferences → local recommendations → experience / quest → reward or memory.
- For travelers: discovery and personalization.
- For hotels: extended guest experience and partner value.
- For local providers: visibility with relevant travelers.
- Trust: clear partner, pricing, privacy, and availability information.
- Call to action: explore ATLAS or discuss a partnership.

### Web design rules

- Use the Path System only as a supporting layer. Keep headings concise, make actions explicit, and ensure that the main message is readable at first glance. Test layout on mobile as well as desktop.


## 15. Physical materials and production

Keep applications feasible for a startup to produce, while maintaining high-quality spacing and material choices.  

### Recommended materials

- Warm white or warm beige uncoated paper for welcome cards and stationery.
- Deep Teal ink for high-contrast headlines and signature elements.
- Muted Bronze as a restrained printed accent where cost and production quality justify it.
- Digital-first collateral when print is not necessary.

### Optional premium production

- Foil, embossing, and specialty stocks may be used for limited hotel or event materials, but the core identity must work in standard CMYK printing and one-color reproduction.

### Email signature

- Name · Role · ATLAS
- Website / social link
- All trips leave a story.
- Keep email signatures compact; avoid large logos, complex patterns, or multiple accent colors.


## 16. Hotel partnership materials

Hotel-facing communication should be more operational than consumer advertising. Emphasize guest experience, ease of implementation, local relevance, and measurable learning from a pilot — without promising results that have not been demonstrated.  

### Suggested structure

- The guest problem: travelers often do not know what to do with available time.
- The ATLAS experience: QR entry, preferences, personalized recommendations, quests, and partner rewards.
- Hotel role: trusted introduction and optional rewards.
- Local partner role: provide experience details, availability, and fulfillment.
- Pilot plan: agreed scope, responsibilities, reporting, and feedback loop.
- Data handling: explain what is collected and how it is used.

### Partnership language

Say “designed to help hotels extend the guest experience” until pilot evidence supports stronger quantified claims. Never present a partnership offer or hotel interest as a signed partnership unless it has been confirmed.  


## 17. Investor presentation application

Investor material should use the same design system but prioritize clarity over decoration.  
**Recommended story order:** problem → insight → product → traveler journey → hotel and local-provider value → business model → pilot plan → evidence and learning → roadmap → team.  
Use charts, metrics, and traction statements only when supported by actual data. Keep Journey Lines as an occasional section marker or flow cue.

## 20. Brand governance

One shared source of truth should control the final logo master, the three core palette values, fonts, pattern assets, and reusable templates.  

### Version control

- Keep source logo artwork read-only.
- Keep derived logo variants in a named asset folder with version numbers.
- Store editable master files separately from exported PDFs and social media images.
- When a rule changes, update the brand book and templates at the same time.
- Ensure partners and agencies receive the same current asset pack.

### Ownership of consistency

- One designated brand owner should approve changes to the logo, palette, typography, and pattern geometry. Local campaign creativity is welcome within the system, but it should not silently introduce a new logo, palette, or typography style.


## 21. Brand consistency checklist


**Before any public release, check the following**

The final ATLAS symbol is used without distortion.  
The orbit remains open and the bronze star remains in the correct position.  
**The official slogan is spelled exactly:** All trips leave a story.  
Deep Teal is #0a4a4f, Warm Beige is #d7c4b1, and Muted Bronze is #a58d66 in supporting brand surfaces.  
Logo artwork colors are not arbitrarily modified.  
English and Georgian fonts render all needed characters.  
Text and controls have sufficient contrast and readable sizes.  
The Path System supports layout instead of competing with it.  
Photography is authentic, respectful, and relevant to the experience.  
QR codes are scannable and have a clear instruction and URL fallback.  
Prices, reward terms, partner status, and claims are accurate.  
The design works at mobile size and in print.  
No unsupported partnership, statistic, testimonial, or performance claim is presented as fact.

# Atlas guest experience master prompt

Copy this prompt when planning or implementing the guest-facing Atlas product. It is self-contained and uses the current Atlas product brief and repository as its source of truth.

---

## Role and objective

You are the product designer, UX engineer, and full-stack developer responsible for the guest-facing Atlas experience. Build a hotel-connected local experience platform that turns a guest's available time into realistic, personalized, explorable activities around the hotel where they are staying.

The product promise is: **Tell us when you're free. We'll figure out what you can do.**

Work in the existing Atlas repository. Before changing code, inspect the current implementation and its uncommitted changes, read `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md`, and read the installed Next.js documentation under `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` for any framework API you will touch. Preserve the existing Mapbox, PostgreSQL/Drizzle, Auth.js, and domain-service architecture. Do not replace or duplicate working domain logic just to reproduce a screen.

## Product contract

Atlas is hotel-first, map-first, time-aware, and partner-controlled. The hotel is the guest's starting point and trusted source for local recommendations. Atlas is not a hotel search engine, a review site, a generic business directory, or a general tourism chatbot.

**Hard eligibility rule:** an outside business is recommendable only when it is a published, verified, non-archived canonical `Place` with an active, confirmed relationship with the exact hotel context and valid at the intended visit time. A nearby, popular, highly rated, or Mapbox-listed place is not eligible by itself. A relationship with Hotel A never applies to Hotel B. Enforce this rule in server-side queries and domain services for maps, search, the Advisor, itineraries, missions, chat, events, and any future offers or bookings. Client filtering is not authorization. For the current Advisor, preserve its 5,000-metre maximum candidate radius from the hotel and its selected-category filter; do not silently widen the radius.

Hotel-owned facilities and hotel events are separate from external partnerships. Facilities may be eligible for that hotel's guests without an external partnership. Do not add an external business to an itinerary or mission when no eligible partner fits; explain the result and offer the guest a way to adjust time or interests.

## Guest identity and stay context

The primary entry is a hotel-specific QR code, and guests must be able to start without conventional sign-up. A QR proves only which hotel context the guest selected; it does not prove a reservation or hotel occupancy. Collect stay dates as guest-declared unless a supported reservation ticket or hotel integration verifies them, and label that verification state accurately in the UI and data.

Represent guest ownership explicitly with a server-side `GuestPrincipal` that can belong to an anonymous guest session or a later linked account. Use an opaque, high-entropy session token in a secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie; store only its hash; rotate and expire it under a documented retention policy. Adapt stays, itineraries, preferences, visits, and achievements to this principal rather than creating fake email/password users. Inspect existing foreign keys and implement this as an additive, backfillable migration: preserve existing guest history, avoid rewriting every table in one destructive migration, and use a deliberate compatibility/backfill sequence. Link an Auth.js account to the same principal only through an explicit, authorized flow. Tell guests when progress is tied to the current device/session and do not promise recovery or account transfer unless implemented. Keep staff authentication and `admin` authorization separate. Resolve hotel identity only from a validated hotel-entry QR or an allowlisted test fixture; never trust a hotel or guest ID supplied by the browser.

**Test-user exception:** in local/development and QA/staging only, an explicitly allowlisted test account must be able to enter a configured seeded test hotel without scanning a hotel QR. Guard this with server-side checks on every request using an explicit Atlas deployment-environment setting (for example, `ATLAS_DEPLOYMENT_ENV`) plus the account allowlist and fixture mapping; fail closed when the setting is absent or unknown. Do not rely only on `NODE_ENV`, since QA/staging deployments may run framework production mode. Use isolated fictional fixtures and never accept an arbitrary client-supplied hotel ID. Production must always require the normal hotel QR flow, including for accounts labeled “test.” No query parameter, client flag, hidden button, or production staff role may activate the bypass.

The guest flow is:

1. In normal use, scan the hotel's entry QR or enter its code. In local/development and QA/staging, an allowlisted test account may open a configured seeded test stay without scanning.
2. Resolve the hotel context. Collect guest-declared stay dates or verified reservation dates, and preserve which source was used.
3. Let the guest choose interests, activity pace, a bounded distance preference up to the current 5 km cap, a supported spending tier, and one free-time window. Allow skipping optional preferences.
4. Show relevant hotel facilities first, followed by only that hotel's approved external partners.
5. Generate a route that fits the selected free time, existing commitments, travel time, visit duration, and return-to-hotel buffer.
6. Keep one itinerary and one Mapbox instance. Let the guest start navigation and complete stops using the configured QR, GPS, or AR check-in method.
7. Record verified visits once, award at most one configured achievement per guest per Place, and show progress and history.
8. Let the guest return during the stay to adjust dates, preferences, or availability and explicitly regenerate the route.

## Guest surfaces and interactions

Inspect both the [Atlas Figma design](https://www.figma.com/design/xGqcfYil4ZxW9gRdBCNfbU/Untitled?node-id=1-672&t=e8n3aei0QyBaJdKU-0) and its [interactive prototype](https://www.figma.com/proto/xGqcfYil4ZxW9gRdBCNfbU/Untitled?node-id=1-948&t=ydJkzeYFsHavLtOo-0&scaling=min-zoom&content-scaling=fixed&page-id=0%3A1). Inspect the actual frames and interactions before implementation; do not infer missing screens from this prompt. If Figma access is unavailable, say so and request an export or access rather than claiming the design was inspected. Keep the map as the environment behind lightweight sheets instead of sending guests into a disconnected application for every action.

The Figma reference includes a map home, hotel QR scan, stay-date selection, preference controls, profile and trip history, settings, mission progress, notifications, an Advisor conversation sheet, Place details, and an achievement-unlocked overlay. Treat them as connected states of the same stay. Use the screens as interaction and visual references; keep every claim and control connected to real Atlas data and available server operations. There is no admin-console design in this Figma file, so design admin workflows as a separate responsive operational interface.

- **Map home:** full-viewport map, hotel and guest location, eligible places, itinerary stops, selected-place details, and navigation. Show the active hotel's eligible facilities and approved external partners. Search may search Atlas's eligible catalog; Mapbox may render the map and calculate routes but must never add recommended Places. Render a 3D model only when its linked canonical Place is eligible for the active hotel (or is that hotel's facility) and otherwise suppress it; a model's lock state is not permission to reveal a partner. A model may have a visual display anchor away from its linked Place. The canonical Place pin remains at the Place's real coordinates; tapping a displaced model opens its linked Place and clearly shows the real Place location and distance. Navigation and check-in always use canonical Place coordinates, never the visual model anchor.
- **Top utility bar:** notifications, guest/stay identity with dates, and the stay assistant. Each opens a compact panel or sheet and can be dismissed without losing map state.
- **Bottom navigation:** map, missions/collection, a prominent central QR scanner, and profile. Provide clear selected states, large touch targets, keyboard access, and safe-area spacing.
- **Hotel QR scanner:** request camera permission only after the guest chooses to start scanning. If permission is denied or unavailable, provide manual code entry. Validate scanned content on the server and explain invalid, expired, revoked, or wrong-purpose codes without leaking token details.
- **Stay dates:** editable check-in and check-out dates with a clear active day. Date changes must be validated and persisted through an authorized server operation.
- **Advisor:** ask when the guest is free, then return a timed itinerary with eligible locations, walking estimates, visit durations, and a return buffer. Treat the free-time duration as a time window, never as a financial budget. Apply selected categories, activity pace, and the guest's bounded distance preference. Add a simple canonical `priceLevel` to Places before using spending tiers; an unknown price must remain unknown and must not be described as affordable. Eligibility and time constraints are hard filters before preference ranking. Opening hours or availability may be used only when Atlas stores a source and last-verified time; otherwise state that the information is unknown and do not claim the place will be open. Keep generation and repair explicit. Never silently replace completed stops or regenerate while the guest is navigating.
- **Itinerary route on the map:** after the guest explicitly generates a plan, show the complete route geometry and all stops for that active itinerary on the existing shared map, with numbered markers, hotel origin and return, and the schedule shown in the itinerary sheet. This is the requested route-preview behavior and intentionally replaces the current “future stops stay hidden” rule for that generated itinerary. Do not reveal routes before generation or other itineraries. Keep check-in progression server-controlled: only the first eligible unvisited stop can be checked in. Reuse the full route geometry already returned and persisted by the itinerary optimizer; do not make a second Mapbox request solely to draw the same line. Bound itinerary stop count to the configured routing provider's documented waypoint limit; if a plan would exceed it, shorten it with an explanation or split it into an explicitly separate plan—never silently drop stops. If geometry is missing or Directions fails, show ordered stops and timings, label geometry unavailable, and offer retry. Route preview is available only while the guest owns the active stay and itinerary.
- **Experience detail:** show verified Place information, hotel/partner context, category, location, expected duration, availability only when it is real, and actions to show it on the map or add it through the itinerary workflow. Do not fabricate ratings, prices, availability, reservations, or partner status.
- **Missions and task completion:** implement missions as persisted place-based tasks generated from guest interests, activity pace, available time, prior completions, and the same hard hotel eligibility rule. A mission may link to an itinerary stop so its task and route stay coherent; completing the task still requires a valid check-in. Every generated mission set must include at least one eligible, real hotel-owned activity. If none fits or is configured, explain the data/schedule gap and do not present an incomplete set as valid. Do not overwhelm the guest; keep task counts bounded and allow an occasional eligible activity outside the guest's usual interests. Place visits require the configured QR or GPS check-in; AR is an additional camera-match check and must also pass GPS evidence. For visual matching, use a validated perceptual descriptor or feature-matching method with a defined similarity threshold; do not compare cryptographic hashes as if different camera captures should be byte-identical. Process camera frames locally where feasible, do not upload raw images by default, and provide QR/GPS fallback when camera matching is unavailable or unsupported. Persist check-ins and task completion idempotently, show progress, and never grant completion from a tap alone. Keep task completion and visit completion per stay distinct from the permanent one-time achievement unlock per guest and Place.
- **Achievements and profile:** show unlocked achievements, verified visit history, stay context, preferences, saved items only where persisted, and trip summaries only when the source data exists. A Place unlock is permanent and cannot be awarded again by rescanning.
- **Notifications:** surface meaningful hotel events, mission updates, and stay reminders from real product data. Keep the feed quiet; do not imply push delivery unless it is implemented and configured.
- **Assistant:** answer questions using the active stay, current preferences, eligible partner network, current itinerary, and supported Atlas data. It must use the same server eligibility service as the rest of the product and say when it lacks reliable information. Do not fabricate weather, event, price, booking, or availability data.

## Visual direction

Match the Figma's warm, friendly map UI: warm cream surfaces (`#F7F3E2`), a quiet stone border (`#D7D3C5`), deep green-black text (`#17211B`), restrained navy-blue actions, pale blue-green map context, and small lime accents for active or achievement states. The file uses Inter and large rounded sheets; preserve the soft 40 px outer sheet/frame language on the mobile surfaces while keeping controls and inner elements more compact. Use the map, not decoration, as the main visual texture.

Sheets should feel light and anchored, with clear hierarchy, readable type, concise copy, visible close/drag affordances, and internal scrolling when needed. At larger viewports, keep the map expansive and move details into a bounded side panel or bottom sheet without stretching text across the screen. Do not let navigation cover map controls or important content. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, browser safe-area insets, keyboard navigation, visible focus, color contrast, and WCAG 2.2 AA targets.

## Data, security, and reliability

- Keep one canonical `Place`, one hotel system, one account/auth system, one PostgreSQL database, one shared Mapbox map, one location service, and one routing service.
- Keep business rules in server-side domain services and repositories. Validate every Server Action and Route Handler as if it were a public API. Authenticate and authorize each mutation; verify the guest session and stay ownership at the data boundary.
- Scope map, Advisor, itinerary, mission, assistant, and check-in data from the server-derived active stay. Ignore or reject client-supplied hotel/guest identity that conflicts with it.
- Enforce the test-user no-QR exception only on local/development and QA/staging, using an explicit server-side Atlas deployment-environment allowlist, test-account allowlist, and seeded hotel fixtures. Fail closed for missing or unknown deployment values; do not rely only on `NODE_ENV`. Exercise the same stay ownership and eligibility checks as normal entry. Deny the bypass in production even if a test account or client request asks for it.
- Use purpose-specific opaque QR tokens, store only hashes, support expiry and revocation, reveal newly issued raw values only once, and never log them. Hotel-entry codes must not be accepted as Place check-in codes.
- Treat hotel QR as hotel-context selection, not reservation verification. Mark stay dates as guest-declared unless ticket/PMS verification exists. Do not promise occupancy validation from a QR code.
- Verify GPS evidence server-side using freshness, accuracy, distance, and the configured check-in policy, while documenting that browser GPS is best-effort evidence and can be unavailable or manipulated. AR requires both a validated visual-match result and the same accepted GPS evidence; a cryptographic hash or client assertion alone is never check-in proof. Keep reference data server-side, process camera frames locally where feasible, rate-limit sensitive operations, and provide a non-AR supported check-in fallback. Make check-ins and unlocks idempotent under retries and concurrent requests.
- Authorize a full itinerary preview against the server-derived guest principal, active stay, and itinerary ownership. Return only the active itinerary's stops and stored route geometry after that plan is explicitly generated. The route preview is the approved exception to progressive reveal; check-in ordering and other guests' data remain protected.
- Minimize collection and retention of precise location and guest data. Request geolocation only when it helps the current action; the map must remain usable when the guest denies location access.
- Do not invent production Places, partners, verification claims, or events. Development fixtures must be clearly fictional and isolated from production.
- Bookings, reservations, payments, and commission claims are outside the current implementation scope unless an already approved provider and business terms exist in the repository. Do not imply these transactions are available.

## Performance and responsive requirements

The app must remain usable on supported modern mobile and desktop browsers, including weak networks and low-memory devices; do not claim support for hardware or browsers lacking WebGL. Maintain one Mapbox map instance per mounted experience. Provide a usable list/map fallback when WebGL is unavailable and permission-denied fallbacks for GPS/camera. Use GeoJSON sources and Mapbox style layers for POI groups rather than a DOM marker per Place. Keep sources small, cluster dense points at low zoom, load 3D models selectively, and avoid map recreation, polling, continuous animation loops, unnecessary React renders, and eager loading of camera/AR code. A custom 3D layer may request repaint after model load/unload or a state change, but must not unconditionally request another frame on every render. Preserve layers through style changes. Define measurable mobile performance targets for the supported device/browser matrix before claiming optimization.

## Definition of done

The complete guest experience in this prompt works with persisted Atlas data: hotel QR entry and the restricted test bypass, anonymous guest ownership, stay dates and their verification source, preference-aware Advisor results, itinerary generation and full map route preview, missions and completion, sequential verified visits, achievements, profile/history, notifications, assistant, eligible 3D models, and supported AR fallback behavior. An allowlisted test account can enter a seeded hotel without QR only in local/development and QA/staging; a production request cannot activate the bypass. A guest cannot see an unapproved business or model by manipulating client input. Preferences influence recommendations without bypassing eligibility or schedule constraints. Route geometry and all stops appear only for the guest's explicitly generated, owned active itinerary. Place-based tasks can be completed only through configured verification and persist once. Map and overlays work at phone, tablet, and desktop sizes; location, camera, storage, and provider failures have honest usable fallbacks; route edits require guest intent; achievements cannot be duplicated; and no feature claims success when its service is unconfigured. Add or update focused automated tests for hotel-specific eligibility, preference ranking, test-environment isolation, route constraints and rendering data, mission generation/completion, check-in verification, and idempotency. Document any functionality that truly depends on unavailable credentials or user-supplied Figma exports. Do not connect to or migrate a remote database without explicit approval.

Start by mapping these requirements to the existing Atlas routes, schemas, queries, services, and UI, and inspect the Figma design and prototype linked above. Identify existing capabilities and confirmed gaps before editing. In the current repository, verify these known audit points rather than assuming they are fixed: external Places must be filtered by the active hotel's confirmed relationship; preference categories may be used while budget and activity-pace fields may not yet affect itinerary generation; mission records and completion may not exist as a complete system; the full itinerary route may not yet be drawn on the map after generation; and independent 3D anchors may be missing. Implement the complete requested guest scope in this task, in the dependency order needed by the codebase. Do not stop after an MVP subset or wait for approval between feature areas. Preserve unrelated working-tree changes, follow repository safety requirements for remote databases and paid services, keep the existing stack, and report exactly what was implemented and actually verified. If Figma remains inaccessible, continue from this prompt's written design specification and identify which visual details need supplied exports; do not claim to have inspected the file.
